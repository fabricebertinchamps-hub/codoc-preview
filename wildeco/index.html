Healthcare · UAE · 2020

NMC Health

Role
Chief Restructuring Officer (newly created post)
Scale
US$6.6bn · 75 facilities · 80+ lenders
Firm at the time
Independent

Appointed Chief Restructuring Officer of NMC Health in March 2020, in a newly created post, to take charge of the group’s debts. NMC was then the UAE’s largest private healthcare operator and was listed on the London Stock Exchange.

Debt was estimated at US$6.6bn across 75 facilities with more than 80 financial institutions, including US$800m of newly identified debt and about US$400m borrowed without board approval.

The English court placed the plc into administration in April 2020. The group later went through ADGM administration and a deed of company arrangement, and emerged in March 2022 under creditor ownership.

Sources: The National, 24 Mar 2020 · Gulf News, 24 Mar 2020 · The National, 25 Mar 2022
Construction · Dubai · 2020–21

Arabtec Holding

Role
Named liquidator by shareholder resolution; adviser on options and liquidation
Scale
AED 1.46bn accumulated losses
Firm at the time
AlixPartners

Arabtec was the UAE’s largest listed construction company.

After a first-half 2020 loss of AED 794m pushed accumulated losses to AED 1.46bn, over half its share capital, shareholders voted on 30 September 2020 to liquidate the company and four subsidiaries, naming AlixPartners and Matthew Wilde as liquidator. The resolution was ratified on 30 November 2020.

In 2021 the group moved into court bankruptcy proceedings under the UAE Bankruptcy Law.

Sources: Gulf News, 30 Sep 2020 · Consultancy-ME, 27 Oct 2020 · Khaleej Times, 21 Jun 2021
Family group · Abu Dhabi · 2019

Al Jaber Group

Role
Chief Restructuring Officer, June to December 2019
Scale
US$1.6bn (AED 5.9bn) · about 20 banks and funds
Firm at the time
Independent

The Abu Dhabi construction, engineering, heavy transport and hotels group restructured US$4.5bn of debt in 2014, then missed a payment in March 2018.

Terms for a second restructuring of about US$1.6bn required a new board with two independent members, a Chief Restructuring Officer and a family member as chairman.

They extended maturities to December 2026, cut the margin from Libor +400bp to +250bp, and combined a two-year interest grace period with asset sales, shareholder support and a new bonding line.

Sources: Gulf News, Dec 2018 · Gulf News, 2019

The Saudi construction group closed on 31 July 2017 with debts of more than SAR 40bn, over 6,000 creditor claims and about 8,000 staff, many unpaid for months. Banks’ exposure was about SAR 13bn.

The Saudi state set up a claims committee in November 2018. Matthew advised the Ministry of Finance.

Sources: Gulf Business, 2019
Sovereign · Dubai · 2014–15

Dubai World

Role
Decree 57 nominee; prepared the filing for five group companies
Scale
US$14.6bn · about 80 creditors
Firm at the time
PwC

In 2014 Dubai World engaged PwC to plan and prepare a Decree 57 filing for five group companies: Dubai World, Dubai World Group Finance, Istithmar World Holdings, Istithmar World PJSC and Port & Free Zone World.

In January 2015 Dubai World filed a voluntary arrangement notification with creditor support above the 67% threshold, to amend its 2011 restructuring: early repayment of US$2.92bn, 2018 maturities extended to 2022, higher pricing and more collateral.

In February 2015 every creditor signed up, so the Decree 57 backstop was withdrawn and the deal closed consensually.

Sources: Gulf Business, 12 Jan 2015 · Gulf News, 2015
Marine · Dubai · 2012

DryDocks World

Role
Decree 57 nominee
Scale
US$2.2bn of syndicated debt and hedging
Firm at the time
PwC

Dubai World’s shipbuilding and ship-repair subsidiary defaulted in August 2011 on US$2.2bn of syndicated debt and hedging.

On 1 April 2012 two DryDocks companies filed under Decree 57, the special regime created in 2009 for Dubai World and its subsidiaries: the first use of the regime, with a parallel Singapore scheme of arrangement.

The tribunal sanctioned the arrangement in August 2012, binding the roughly 2% of holdouts. The press later called it “a positive test case for UAE bankruptcy reform”.

Sources: The National, 2 Apr 2012 · Al-Mirsal, 28 Aug 2012 · The National, 19 May 2014
